Riding Jackets for Gulf Heat: Mesh, Armour, and What to Actually Look For

By Nandkishor Sharma · 15 September 2026

Full mesh isn't a compromise on protection — it's the only sensible choice for most of the riding year here. Here's what separates a jacket that protects you from one that just looks like it does.

1. Mesh coverage, not just mesh panels

  • A jacket with mesh only on the chest and a solid back still traps heat — look for mesh across the whole torso and arms
  • Perforated leather is a middle ground — better airflow than solid leather, more abrasion resistance than mesh
  • Check the lining too — a waterproof liner sewn in permanently blocks airflow even on a mesh outer

2. CE-rated armour is non-negotiable

  • Shoulder and elbow armour should carry a CE Level 1 or 2 rating — Level 2 absorbs more impact energy
  • Back protectors are often sold separately — don't skip this, it's the most commonly missing piece
  • Armour should sit exactly over the joint when the jacket is zipped, not shift with movement
Mesh keeps you riding through summer; armour is what a mesh jacket is actually for.

3. Fit for riding position, not standing

  • Try the jacket in a riding crouch, not just standing straight — sleeves should stay covering your wrists
  • A slightly longer back hem prevents gap at the waist when leaned forward
  • Adjustable straps at the waist and arms let the armour stay positioned as the jacket ages

Quick checklist before buying

  • Mesh coverage across torso and arms, not just chest panels
  • CE Level 1 or 2 armour at shoulders and elbows
  • Back protector included or budgeted separately
  • Fit tested in a riding crouch, not standing

A good mesh jacket disappears once you're moving — you stop noticing the heat and stop thinking about the armour. That's the actual goal.
