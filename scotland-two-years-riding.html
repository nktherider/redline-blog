Two Years on Two Wheels: Riding Scotland's Roads (2015–2017)

By Nandkishor Sharma · 12 June 2017

Before Dubai's heat and highways, there were two years of riding Scotland's roads — a completely different kind of challenge. Where UAE riding tests you against heat, Scotland tests you against everything else: cold, rain, single-track roads, and sheep that do not care about your right of way.

1. The roads themselves are the reward

Scotland's driving/riding roads don't need a destination to justify the ride — the A82 along Loch Lomond, the sweeping runs through Glencoe, and sections of what's now called the North Coast 500 are worth riding purely for the road itself.

  • Single-track roads with passing places demand a different kind of awareness than anything in city riding — blind crests hide oncoming traffic constantly
  • Livestock on the road is a real, frequent hazard in the Highlands, not a rare warning sign — sheep move unpredictably and often in groups
  • Weather can change within a single ride — what starts dry at the coast can turn to rain and fog by the time you reach higher ground

2. Cold and wet riding is a skill you have to build

  • Layering matters more than any single piece of "warm" gear — a proper base layer under waterproofs beats one thick jacket
  • Heated grips stopped being a luxury and became close to essential for anything beyond an hour in Scottish winter conditions
  • Visor fogging in cold, damp air is a constant fight — a pinlock insert made a bigger difference than any anti-fog spray
Scotland doesn't test your speed — it tests your patience, your layering, and your ability to read weather that changes by the mile.

3. What two years of Scottish riding actually taught me

  • Reading a road's surface became second nature — wet cattle grids, mossy patches, and loose gravel on single tracks all needed constant attention
  • Riding within visibility rather than within speed limit — Highland fog can drop visibility to almost nothing within minutes
  • Real patience with slower traffic on single-track roads — there's often nowhere safe to overtake until the next passing place

4. Favourite stretches worth knowing

  • A82 along Loch Lomond — sweeping curves with the loch running alongside for most of the ride
  • Glencoe — dramatic elevation and some of the most photographed riding roads in Scotland, best ridden early to avoid tourist traffic
  • Applecross Pass (Bealach na Ba) — steep hairpins and one of the highest road climbs in the UK, not for a first ride in bad weather

Moving to UAE riding after two years in Scotland felt like learning a new sport — heat instead of cold, wide highways instead of single tracks. But the fundamentals Scotland taught me about reading conditions and riding within what you can actually see have never stopped being useful, wherever the ride happens to be.
