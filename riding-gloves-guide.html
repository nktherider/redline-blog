Riding Gloves: What Separates a Good Pair From a Bad One

By Nandkishor Sharma · 17 September 2026

Gloves are the gear riders compromise on most, usually right before the ride where it actually mattered. A few things separate genuinely protective gloves from ones that just look the part.

1. Palm slider and knuckle protection

  • A hard palm slider reduces friction burn in a slide-out — check for it specifically, not just padding
  • Knuckle armour (hard plastic or carbon) should sit directly over the knuckle, not float above it
  • Double-layer palms and reinforced stitching at high-wear points last significantly longer

2. Grip and feel matter as much as protection

  • Pre-curved fingers reduce fatigue on long rides and improve lever feel
  • Too much padding on the fingers reduces clutch and brake feedback — test this before buying, not after
  • Touchscreen-compatible fingertips are a genuine convenience, not just a marketing line

3. Ventilated doesn't mean unprotected

  • Perforated leather or mesh panels on the back of the hand help significantly in UAE heat without sacrificing palm protection
  • A gauntlet cuff (extending past the wrist, worn under the jacket sleeve) prevents gap injuries better than short cuffs
  • Wrist closures should be snug enough that the glove can't be pulled off in a slide

Before you buy

  • Palm slider and knuckle armour confirmed, not assumed
  • Grip and lever feel tested in-store
  • Gauntlet cuff worn under the jacket sleeve, not over it

Hands are usually the first thing to touch down in a fall — gloves are the last piece of gear worth compromising on to save a little money.
