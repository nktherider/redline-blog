7 Best Superbike Routes in the UAE Every Rider Should Experience

By Nandkishor Sharma · 27 September 2026

One thing riding in the UAE has taught me: you don't need to leave the country to find a great motorcycle road. A few hours on the bike can take you from Dubai's wide highways to desert roads, mountain curves, rocky valleys and the East Coast. The UAE is small, but for riders it offers a surprisingly good mix of terrain — the trick is knowing where to go, when to go, and how to prepare.

These are public roads, not racetracks. Ride within the speed limit, hold your lane, respect other road users, and check current road conditions before setting out.

1. Jebel Jais — the classic UAE mountain ride

Ask most UAE riders to name one mountain road and Jebel Jais is usually the first answer. Located in Ras Al Khaimah, the road climbs through the Hajar Mountains with long sweeping corners and elevation changes. At around 1,934 metres, Jebel Jais is the UAE's highest mountain, and the ride up feels like a completely different country from the flat roads around Dubai.

  • Long sweeping corners with genuine mountain scenery and cooler air at elevation
  • Don't let the road quality make you overconfident — look through the corner, hold your line, and expect other vehicles, cyclists and riders sharing the road
  • Early morning is the better window, especially outside the cooler winter months

2. Hatta — the easiest escape from Dubai

For Dubai-based riders, Hatta is one of the simplest ways to leave the city behind. The ride starts on Dubai's highways and gradually shifts into desert and mountain scenery as you approach. The real appeal isn't just the destination — it's the transition, watching the landscape change from buildings and traffic to open desert and mountains.

  • Good combination of highway and mountain riding in one trip
  • Start early — you want to be heading back before serious daytime heat sets in
  • A solid progression ride once you're past purely city riding

3. Jebel Hafeet — for riders who love corners

Jebel Hafeet near Al Ain is a completely different experience from Jebel Jais's long sweepers. The mountain road climbs roughly 11.7km with around 60 corners — one of the most corner-dense rides in the UAE.

  • Not the relaxed first-trip choice, but excellent if you want technical cornering over long straights
  • Focus on smooth braking, correct corner entry, looking through the turn, and smooth throttle application — not outright speed
  • Sunrise or sunset both make for a genuinely beautiful ride here
The best route isn't always the longest or the fastest — it's the one that makes you want to ride it again.

4. Fujairah to Khor Fakkan — mountains meet the coast

If Jebel Jais and Jebel Hafeet are one side of UAE riding, the East Coast is the other. The Fujairah–Khor Fakkan stretch combines mountain scenery with coastal views, making it one of the most visually rewarding rides available. Don't rush this one — take your time, stop for photos, and let the journey matter more than reaching any specific point.

5. Dubai to Al Ain — the long highway run

Not every good ride needs dozens of corners. Sometimes you just want distance. The Dubai–Al Ain direction is a solid long-highway option, and it opens up naturally into a bigger loop rather than a straight there-and-back:

  • Dubai → Al Ain → Jebel Hafeet → Al Ain → Dubai turns a simple highway run into a genuinely interesting full day

6. Al Qudra — the easy Dubai morning ride

When half a day isn't available, Al Qudra is the easiest option — close enough to Dubai for a quick early ride, with long stretches of open desert road. It gets busier with cyclists through the morning, so smooth, predictable riding matters more here than anywhere else on this list.

7. Hatta–Kalba / East Coast loop — the full-day option

With a full day available, combine mountain and coast: Dubai → Hatta → mountain roads → East Coast → Fujairah/Kalba → Dubai. This isn't about following one perfect road — it's about building your own day out of highways, desert, mountains, curves, coastline, and a few good food stops along the way.

Before any of these rides

  • Tyre pressure and condition, chain, engine oil, coolant, brakes, lights and fuel checked
  • Full gear — helmet, gloves, jacket, boots — plus water, phone and navigation sorted
  • For longer loops, someone knows your planned route and rough return time
  • Start early — cooler temperatures, less traffic, better visibility, and a lot less stress on you as the rider

Start with the shorter, easier routes if you're newer to riding here — Al Qudra or a calm Hatta run. As experience builds, the mountain roads and full-day loops open up a side of UAE riding most people never see from the highway. Ride early, ride prepared, and ride within your limits — the road will still be there tomorrow.
